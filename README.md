# Chikhaldara Eco-Tourism Native Stay

**Architecture Thesis Project | 2017–2018**  
**Location:** Paratwada–Chikhaldara Road, Amravati, Maharashtra, India  
**Project Type:** Eco-tourism hospitality / climate-responsive native stay  
**Design Intent:** Comfort over luxury • place-based tourism • local livelihoods • environmental sensitivity

> This repository documents an architecture thesis conceived during 2017–18. The project explored a low-impact eco-tourism stay near Chikhaldara in the Melghat landscape, using climate-responsive planning, vernacular references, contour-sensitive development and local livelihood linkages.

## Project in one sentence

A **hospitality-led eco-tourism settlement** that offers visitors a rooted native stay experience while linking tourism expenditure to local employment, nearby farms, local food and landscape conservation.

## Why this project?

During the project period, Chikhaldara was being positioned as an important tourism destination in the Melghat region. Its hilly terrain, relatively pleasant climate, scenic landscape and proximity to nature-based tourism created an opportunity for a stay experience that could be more locally rooted than a generic resort.

The project therefore asks:

**Can hospitality become a tool for both place-based tourism and local livelihood generation without reproducing a high-footprint luxury-resort model?**

## Core objectives

- Create a **native-style, climate-responsive stay experience** rather than a luxury resort.
- Work with the **hilly site, contours, rainfall, vegetation and views** instead of flattening the terrain.
- Use architectural principles derived from **Melghat/Korku spatial traditions** and monsoon-responsive Indian architecture.
- Generate opportunities for **local employment** in hospitality, food, housekeeping, guiding, maintenance and operations.
- Build livelihood linkages with nearby **coffee, strawberry and other farm-based producers** through farm visits, tasting, local food and produce sales.
- Reduce environmental disturbance through **low-rise, low-footprint planning**, pedestrian movement, rainwater management, tree retention and minimal cut-and-fill.

## Design framework

### 1. PLACE
Forest • contour • rainfall • climate • local architectural identity

### 2. PEOPLE
Local employment • tribal/rural livelihoods • farmers • guides • food and craft networks

### 3. EXPERIENCE
Native stay • nature trails • local cuisine • farm visits • coffee/strawberry experiences • community spaces

### 4. ENVIRONMENTAL LIMIT
Development footprint • biodiversity sensitivity • drainage • carrying capacity • waste and water systems

## Key architectural idea

**Forest → Contour → Rain → Roof → Verandah → Native Stay Unit**

The built form is intended to sit **with the slope rather than against it**. Smaller clusters follow contours, vehicle penetration is limited, pedestrian movement is prioritised and the architecture uses deep roof overhangs, shaded verandahs, protected openings and locally appropriate material expression.

## Hospitality model

The project is **hospitality-first**, with farm and nature experiences acting as supporting livelihood linkages.

### Learning reference: Sula Vineyards

Sula demonstrates how a strong place-based identity can turn a production landscape into a destination through tasting, tours, food, events and hospitality.

The proposed Chikhaldara project reverses that hierarchy:

| Sula Vineyards | Chikhaldara Proposal |
|---|---|
| Wine production is primary | Hospitality is primary |
| Tourism supports the wine destination | Nature, farms and local culture support the stay experience |
| Vineyard tours and tasting | Forest stay, trails, local food, coffee/strawberry and farm experiences |
| Production-led identity | Landscape- and livelihood-led identity |
