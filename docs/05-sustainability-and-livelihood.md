# Sustainability & Livelihood Model

## Environmental Sustainability

The project proposes a low-impact approach through:

- rainwater harvesting;
- greywater treatment and reuse;
- composting;
- waste segregation;
- permeable pathways;
- native planting;
- minimal land disturbance;
- controlled site lighting;
- limited vehicle movement;
- tree retention;
- decentralised environmental services where feasible.

## Biodiversity Position

The project does **not** assume that tourism automatically protects biodiversity.

Instead, the architectural proposition is that a **planned, low-footprint hospitality model** can potentially reduce pressure for scattered, visually insensitive development by:

- concentrating built areas;
- preserving no-build landscape zones;
- protecting drainage;
- limiting visitor capacity;
- retaining vegetation;
- reducing unnecessary road infrastructure;
- linking economic value to landscape quality.

## Local Employment

Potential direct employment includes:

- reception and operations;
- housekeeping;
- food preparation;
- maintenance;
- gardening;
- guiding;
- security;
- transport and local logistics.

## Local Livelihood Linkages

Potential indirect livelihood opportunities include:

- coffee growers;
- strawberry and other farm owners;
- local food suppliers;
- local artisans;
- local construction labour;
- drivers and guides;
- small producers and vendors.

## Visitor-to-Livelihood Chain

**Stay → Local food → Coffee / farm visit → Nature trail → Local produce / craft → Community interaction**

The project therefore tries to keep a larger share of tourism spending within the local economy.
