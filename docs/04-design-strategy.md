# Design & Planning Strategy

## Design Language

The project explores a **contemporary native / vernacular-inspired architecture**.

It does not attempt to replicate a traditional tribal house literally. Instead, it extracts spatial and climatic principles such as:

- otla / threshold;
- verandah / shaded transition;
- compact internal planning;
- strong indoor–outdoor connection;
- sloping roofs;
- locally familiar material character;
- community-oriented cluster planning.

## Climate Response

Key architectural responses include:

- sloping roofs for monsoon rainfall;
- deep roof overhangs;
- protected fenestration;
- shaded verandahs;
- cross ventilation;
- orientation-sensitive openings;
- controlled west exposure;
- high-level ventilation;
- thermal mass where appropriate.

## Terrain Response

- contour-following roads and paths;
- stepped/terraced placement;
- smaller building footprints;
- minimal cut-and-fill;
- distributed clusters instead of large blocks;
- existing vegetation used to visually integrate the development.

## Circulation Hierarchy

### Public
Arrival, parking, reception, dining and local produce.

### Semi-public
Shared court, tasting, gathering and community spaces.

### Private
Native stay clusters.

### Ecological
Forest, drainage, trails and viewpoints.

### Service
Staff, utilities, maintenance, waste and water infrastructure.
