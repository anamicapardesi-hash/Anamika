# Design Timeline | 2017–2018

## 2017 — Topic Exploration

Initial thesis exploration focused on the relationship between:

- architecture and tourism;
- regional identity;
- nature-based hospitality;
- local livelihoods;
- climate-responsive design.

## Late 2017 — Regional Research

The Chikhaldara–Melghat region emerged as a potential study area because of:

- hill-station character;
- scenic forest landscape;
- regional tourism potential;
- nearby tribal/rural communities;
- possibilities for farm-linked tourism;
- strong scope for climate-sensitive architectural design.

## Late 2017 to Early 2018 — Site Selection

A site along the **Paratwada–Chikhaldara road** was selected as the architectural setting.

Selection criteria included:

- accessibility;
- views;
- hilly terrain;
- landscape character;
- proximity to Chikhaldara;
- proximity to local settlements and farms;
- potential for a low-rise clustered development.

## Early 2018 — Case Study and Programme Definition

Experiential tourism references were studied to understand:

- destination identity;
- hospitality programme;
- visitor circulation;
- supporting commercial/food functions;
- local economic spillovers.

**Sula Vineyards** became one learning reference for how a strong destination narrative can link landscape, product, tourism and hospitality.

## 2018 — Concept Generation

The principal design sequence evolved as:

**Forest → Contour → Rain → Roof → Verandah → Stay Unit**

Parallel concepts included:

- preserve natural landscape;
- reduce cut-and-fill;
- use smaller clusters;
- follow contours;
- prioritise pedestrians;
- embed environmental services into the landscape.

## 2018 — Site Planning

The site plan developed through:

- public arrival zone near the road;
- central reception/dining/community area;
- distributed stay clusters;
- limited service access;
- pedestrian and nature-trail network;
- view points;
- rainwater and drainage features;
- conservation/no-build areas.

## 2018 — Unit Development

Stay units were developed around:

- monsoon-responsive roofs;
- deep verandahs;
- shaded fenestration;
- cross ventilation;
- forest views;
- compact planning;
- regional material expression;
- comfort without luxury excess.

## 2018 — Final Thesis Proposal

The final design proposition combined four themes:

### Place
Landscape, climate, terrain and architectural identity.

### People
Local jobs, farm owners, rural/tribal communities and local skills.

### Experience
Native stay, food, trails, farms, culture and nature.

### Environmental Responsibility
Low footprint, water management, controlled circulation, local material logic and landscape retention.
